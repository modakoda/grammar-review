      The vault

      Your words stay yours.

      A tool that reads everything you type has to earn that. Grammar Review is built to see as little as possible, and to keep none of it.

      • Password fields are never read. Terminals, password managers and code editors are off by default, and you can switch off any app.
      • Spelling, consistency and your style guide are checked on the Mac. Text is sent only while AI is on: the paragraphs you changed, the text whose tone you asked about, and what you rewrite or draft.
      • It goes only to the AI service you chose, with your own key, or to your local model server. With a model on your Mac, nothing leaves it at all.
      • Only settings and AI request counts are stored on disk, never text. API keys live in the Keychain.
      • Release builds check the project website for updates about once a day. Nothing about you or your text is sent with it.

      Free and open source. Release builds check for updates once installed.

      SHA-256 checksums

      To check a download, run shasum -a 256 Grammar-Review.dmg in Terminal and compare.

      1. Download and install

        Open the disk image, drag Grammar Review to Applications and open it from there.

        This build isn’t notarized, so macOS stops it the first time: Control-click the app and choose Open, or click Open Anyway in System Settings → Privacy & Security. Once is enough.

      2. Allow Accessibility

        Grammar Review needs it to read and edit text fields. The setup guide sends you to the right switch in System Settings and notices the moment you turn it on.

      3. Choose your AI, or don’t

        Paste an API key for Claude, OpenAI, xAI, Gemini, Mistral or DeepSeek; point it at a local model; or skip it and keep spelling only. Then just start typing.

      Good questions

      Asked before you asked.

      What does it cost?

      The app is free and open source. Spelling, consistency and the style guide cost nothing. AI suggestions use either your own API key, billed by that service for what you use, or a local model, which costs nothing at all.

      Settings → AI → Usage shows this month’s requests, tokens and an estimated cost at the service’s list prices.

      Which apps does it work in?

      Any app that exposes its text to macOS Accessibility: native Mac apps, Safari, Chrome and other Chromium browsers, and Electron apps. When an app refuses edits, Grammar Review pastes the fix instead and restores your clipboard afterwards.

      Canvas-based editors that draw their own text, like Google Docs, don’t expose it, so they get nothing. Where an app reports text positions wrongly, you still get the badge and the panel.

      Does it work in my language?

      Spelling works in every language your Mac has a dictionary for. AI suggestions detect the language automatically, or you can fix it in Settings, and the fix always stays in your language. The large hosted models handle many languages well; small local models are best in English.

      Where does my text go?

      Nowhere, unless AI is on. Then the paragraphs you changed (up to 6,000 characters by default), the text whose tone you asked about, and what you rewrite or draft go to the service you chose with your own key, or to your local model server. Password fields and apps you switched off are never read. Only settings and request counts are saved on disk.

      Can my team share a style guide?

      Yes. Add rules in Settings → Style Guide, then Export them as CSV or JSON. Teammates click Import…; an imported rule replaces one for the same term. Rules are checked offline in every app and win over AI suggestions.

      Does Write with AI make things up?

      It’s told never to invent facts it wasn’t given, like dates, prices, numbers or names. Instead it leaves a placeholder such as [date] for you to fill in. Nothing goes into your text until you press Insert or Replace.

      Why isn’t it in the App Store?

      The App Store sandbox doesn’t allow an app to read and edit text fields in other apps, which is the whole point of Grammar Review.

      macOS says it can’t open the app

      Notarized releases open normally. If a build isn’t notarized, macOS stops it the first time: Control-click the app in Applications and choose Open, or click Open Anyway in System Settings → Privacy & Security. You only need to do this once.

      It keeps asking for Accessibility again

      macOS ties the permission to the app’s code signature, and every unsigned rebuild gets a new one. Click Reset Permission in the setup guide and switch it on again. If you build from source, run scripts/setup-signing.sh once to stop it happening after each rebuild.
